What a venture operating firm is

Common Ground is a venture operating firm. The group puts its work in one plain line: “When a company is starting out or has stalled, we step inside and bring the people, processes, or capital it’s missing.”

Where a private equity fund supplies capital and a consultancy supplies advice, a venture operating firm supplies people, processes and capital together, and takes accountable roles inside the business. The term sits beside three older ones. Like a venture studio, it builds companies from zero. Like a team of operating partners, it steps into companies in transition and runs the parts that are stuck. Like venture capital, it can bring capital, but the capital comes with the people who do the work.

Two modes

  • Build. In the manner of a venture studio, the group starts companies from zero: brand, product, capital and operators from the first day.
  • Scale. As operating partners, the group steps into companies in transition that have stalled or lack a capability in house, and runs brand, capital, execution, legal and AI until the business holds on its own.

How the work runs

  • Name what is missing first. Every engagement starts by naming what the company lacks: the people, the processes, or the capital. The shape of the engagement follows from that answer, so no two are the same.
  • Inside, not alongside. The principals take defined roles inside the business rather than advising from the outside, and stay until the work holds without them.
  • Results are the aim. The group agrees with the client, at the start, what will be measured, from what starting point and by when, and works against that number. Finding the real baseline is usually the first real work of an engagement.
  • Run at home first. Everything Common Ground recommends, it has already run on its own portfolio first, including the AI systems operating inside its own ventures.

What the work includes

Most of the group’s work is building companies and moving companies past a stall. It also takes on defined pieces of work, each one sized to what the client needs:

  • Building and scaling companies. Operating seats inside a company, from brand and go-to-market to capital readiness, execution, technology and legal.
  • Websites. Sites designed, written and built for a company, from the first page to search readiness.
  • Beacon. A read of how a company is found: its site scored for search, generative-engine and answer-engine visibility, with the fixes.
  • Audits. A defined look at one part of a business, such as its technology environment, its operations or its numbers, with each finding paired with its fix.
  • Perspective. A second read from operators on a plan, a deal or a decision before the owner commits to it.
  • Specific products. A tool, system or product built for one client’s need, from estimating engines to client portals.

The group’s own tools for this work are collected as Kinwork. Smaller companies looking for productized AI services are referred to APEXX AI, the group’s AI agency.

Who it works with

Common Ground works with two kinds of company: new companies it builds from zero, and founder-operators running companies at roughly $10M and up in revenue, typically businesses that have hit a ceiling where brand, capital, execution, legal, and AI all need to move at once.
