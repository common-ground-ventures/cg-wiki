Xtrata: a pricing problem, not a lead problem

Work · Brand

Fractional CRO and construction consultant to Xtrata: $48 million of projects bid, a $220 million pipeline, a bid system of record and a parity pitch.

Wiki/Work/Xtrata

What it is

Xtrata, formerly Strata, makes SABS, a patented prefabricated panel wall system, in a factory in Arizona. General contractors and developers liked the system once they saw it installed, and the factory had real capacity.

The company reached Fowler through a third party bringing people with construction reputations into the business. The seat opened as lead generation through his builder and developer relationships, with Lïef Development pricing and bidding installation on the jobs that came in. It grew into a year-long seat across sales, marketing, pricing, brand and go-to-market, with collateral addressing six kinds of work: industrial, quick-service restaurant, hospitality, multifamily, office and education.

What Common Ground did

  • Priced everything the channel sent and read the bids side by side. Quotes ran from a premium over wood framing to near parity with no formula connecting them, so deals were dying at the bid desk, not at the top of the funnel.
  • Made the case to leadership, on the company's own bid data, that pricing rather than lead volume was the problem.
  • Built a bid system of record on Next.js and Supabase: plan-stage tracking, a readiness state on every bid, a stale-contact alert and a read-only share for the manufacturer's team. For the first time 32 bids, 13 of them active at cut-over, sat in one screen, with every bid version, roughly 187 PDFs, archived underneath.
  • Derived the labor-to-material relationship across 14 live bids and turned it into a bundled install rate.
  • Had the rate checked outside the building: an outside architect compared it with real crew output, and the manufacturer's operations lead ran live test plans through his own labor calculator.
  • Moved the pitch from a specialty premium to parity with conventional wood framing: SABS priced within about 5 percent of wood framing at standard material pricing, since an estimator compares every bid to wood on cost per square foot.
  • Mapped where the plan sets came from. A sweep of the archive found 11 of its 28 projects carried the same architect's title block, and on those projects the wall assembly is chosen at schematic design, before any contractor bids, so outreach shifted toward architects and developers.
  • Common Ground advised against the rename from Strata to Xtrata: the company, the system and the pipeline were unchanged, and a new name does not fix the number at the bid desk. Leadership made the call, and the pricing doctrine and channel plan carried through the rename intact.

Results

  • $48 million of actual projects bid, relationships with 30 developers and general contractors across the country, and a $220 million pipeline, with potential national work beyond it.
  • The manufacturer now budgets SABS labor by a metric the seat set, and its own labor calculator landed within 4 percent of the derived rate on each of three test plans, a spread of 6.3 points.
  • The sales pitch moved from a specialty premium to parity with wood framing, answering the estimator's second question before it is asked.
  • The seat grew from a lead-referral arrangement into a year-long engagement across marketing, sales, go-to-market, builder and developer relationships, branding and pricing.
