Wiki/Work/Xtrata/Questions answered

Questions about Xtrata, answered in the words of the people who did the work. The engagement itself is on its own page.

What changed in commercial performance once the pricing basis and parity with wood were in place?

Bid volume went up, and our win rate went up sharply. People needed to see the comparison, and parity with wood was the easy thing for them to understand. Same price, better product, and it is very easy to say yes. It made bidding easy for us too: give us your competitor's bid and we will match it. The narrative got simple. We did not have to talk about everything the system does, just that it is a better-performing structure at the same price you would pay for wood. Simplifying a very complex story was the most effective thing we did.

What made contractors willing to use the system on their projects?

It was not the certification or the training. It was the sales narrative. The pitch to contractors had become confusing, so we cut it down to one line: same price as framing, better product. Once a contractor could follow it and see they were not going to get screwed, they were willing to put it on their projects.

How was installed labor turned into a rate the bid desk could use?

Hours and training. We counted both and tracked the data, and it was very simple. Once you have crews in training, it is easy to say these people did this much work in this amount of time, compared to how long traditional framing would have taken for the same space.

What did the bid data say about the sales problem?

It came in as a lead-generation ask, and the bid data said the diagnosis was wrong. With every bid the channel had produced side by side, pricing ran from a premium over wood framing to near parity with no formula connecting one quote to the next, so deals were not dying for lack of leads; they were dying at the bid desk, at the estimator's second question. We put every bid into one system of record, derived a rate from the company's own bids, had it checked against the manufacturer's own labor calculator, and then changed the pitch to parity: same price as framing, better product.

What followed once general contractors were trained and certified?

Bid volume went up once the pitch was one line, and the mix changed. Before, most of the work was owner-builders, people putting up their own houses because the product is easy to use, and the company was not set up for contractors. Certification is the measure we would point to, because a general contractor who gets certified is choosing to bid it on his own jobs.

Which trades took to the block system best?

Masons and stucco crews took to the block system best, and drywallers after them. That is who we trained first.

What showed that trade skills were transferring once crews began installing?

Trial and error, simply. We put people on the work and watch how fast they pick it up. Masonry, framing and stucco carry the same habits into concrete and drywall, so all five count.
