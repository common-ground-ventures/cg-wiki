H.E.E.A.L. Santa Barbara: building a nonprofit around the easy yes

Work · Operating

Volunteer president of a Santa Barbara grief and loss nonprofit, building its outreach, fundraising and board governance around the people who refer families.

Wiki/Work/H.E.E.A.L. Santa Barbara

What it is

H.E.E.A.L. Santa Barbara was a new nonprofit that helped people through grief and loss with exercise and the arts, as an alternative to clinical talk therapy.

Jesse Fowler held the president's seat as a volunteer while running Tellus Design and Build. The job was fundraising, governance and partnerships with schools and care providers.

What Common Ground did

  • Saw that grieving families rarely find programs on their own. A school counselor or a hospice coordinator hands them one, in a short conversation at a full desk, and that person was the real customer.
  • Built the outreach around what a referral costs the person giving it: lead with the three facts a counselor can repeat without checking anything, that the program was free, local and had no waitlist, and put the board, the model and the funders behind the yes.
  • Treated grants, donor relationships and referral outreach as one record, so the same governance and funding story served a grant committee and a school, and fundraising stopped being a separate track from outreach.
  • Took the president off the Accountable line where a partner would look. The board took accountability for financial reporting and public communications, a treasurer or finance lead took responsibility for the reporting, and larger grants went to board sign-off, so the person raising the money was not the only check on it.
  • Spent volunteer hours on individual counselors and coordinators rather than districts or hospital systems, and measured the second referral from the same person, not the first.

Results

  • Four years and four months in the seat, through organizational growth, fundraising expansion and stronger board governance.
  • A public recommendation of the board's leadership in December 2010, from someone who sat in on board meetings that first year.
  • The easy-yes test any service that depends on a gatekeeper can reuse: name who actually says yes, write down what a yes costs them, put the facts that need no checking first, put the credibility behind it, and measure the second referral.
