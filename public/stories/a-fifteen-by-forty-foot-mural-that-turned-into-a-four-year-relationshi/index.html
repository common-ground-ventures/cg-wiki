Wiki/Stories/A fifteen-by-forty-foot mural that turned into a four-year relationship

The relationship with a performance car brand's California hub started small. A single wall mural with wraparound racing stripes and a signature. Instead of treating the mural as a one off job, I used it to earn trust for the next piece of work, then the next, building out the store, and eventually the 3,000 square foot Snake Pit VIP lounge inside Carroll Shelby's former apartment. Big scopes rarely get handed to you on the first job. Small work done exceptionally well is the actual audition for the work that matters, and the discipline is treating the small job with the same seriousness as the big one you're hoping it leads to. The relationship grew from a mural to a retail store to a full VIP lounge with structural modifications, roll up doors, and a bar, over four years. The co-CEO's own words: the team earned their trust. Nobody hands you the marquee project first. You earn it one small, well executed job at a time. It compounded because a real person watched my team do small things right, again and again, before he ever trusted us with something irreplaceable.
