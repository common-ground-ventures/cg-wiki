Wiki/Stories/Banning round numbers from the bid

A residential bid needed a defensible price per square foot story, and the fast, simple math, total price divided by livable square footage, was spitting out a number that felt too low and wouldn't hold up next to a comparable project.

I standardized on one denominator, conditioned under roof, for every price comparison. Tagged every cost line to an explicit, defensible basis, and stripped the round numbers and vague language out of anything client facing.

A vague square footage number invites exactly the challenge it got. The fix was structural, define the denominator once, apply it everywhere, and make every line show its own logic. A porta john is a monthly rate tied to duration, not a guess. A patio allowance is a stated percentage, not a hidden extra. And honestly, that same discipline doubles as the sales pitch, pricing that's genuinely all inclusive differentiates you against competitors who bury their costs.

I rebuilt the line items against that logic. Architectural design at a fixed rate per square foot, structural fixed separately, patio allowances standardized as a percentage of livable space, and I flagged explicitly that if the comparable project's own takeoff ever turns out wrong, everything downstream of it is wrong too.

A bid is only as credible as its denominator. Pick one, defend it, and never let a client facing number be a round guess.

I built a system that couldn't quietly hide a bad assumption, because I don't trust myself, or anyone else, to remember to check it by hand every time.
