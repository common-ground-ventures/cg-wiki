Wiki/Stories/Running the deal review before the investor does

A landowner in Yavapai County spent twenty-five years putting together 344 acres with nine permitted wells, and asked Common Ground into every part of the project: the financials, the lender package, the raise, the buyers and the brand. When his investor and lender deck came to us, the decision was what kind of help to give.

A page of slide comments would have been the polite answer. I didn't think it was the useful one. Any serious investor or lender is going to run their own review before they take a meeting, so we ran it first, on our side of the table, while there was still time to fix the deck. We rebuilt his forecast from the cells and ran it month by month the way a construction lender would. Seven reviewers each read it from one seat: the investor, the lender, the family buying a home there, water and entitlements, cost and schedule, appraisal and absorption, brand and story. One reviewer's only job was to break it. An independent second check went over every number, a final reviewer threw out anything that didn't trace to a slide, a cell or a public source, and then we checked the law ourselves.

That last step earned its place. Three of our reviewers agreed the state's water rule could stop lot sales. The statute said otherwise. The county never adopted the mandatory rule, so a water finding gets disclosed to buyers instead of stopping the subdivision, and refundable reservations are allowed before the public report. We would have handed him a red flag that wasn't real and taken away his best legal way to prove demand early.

The first round of notes went out in early October. He came back nine days later with a deck that took them: three scenarios side by side, a real risks slide, the full well table, the amenities budget carried in the model. The second round went after the questions a lender asks on day one, how the homes get funded while they are being built and how the investors get paid before the sales arrive, and every finding came with the fix next to it.

Nothing has closed yet. What I trust is that he takes the notes and ships the next version. Telling someone the hard part early, with the fix beside it, is how you stay in the deal.
