The bug he built the gate for

Wiki/Stories/The bug he built the gate for

An early version of the estimating engine shipped a bid for a quick-service restaurant with a markup calculation error baked into the pricing. An honest mistake, but one that could have cost a client real money if nobody caught it.

My team caught it, corrected it in a revised version, and instead of just quietly patching it and moving on, we used the failure to design the permanent verification gates the engine still runs today.

A caught mistake is a relief. A mistake that changes how the whole system checks itself afterward is worth more than the discomfort of admitting it happened. The right response to an internal failure is making the system structurally incapable of repeating it, not just fixing that one instance.

The corrected bid went out at the right number. And the incident is the direct reason the engine now runs twenty-four hard assertions that fail the build automatically instead of letting a bad number reach a client.

A mistake caught before it reaches a client isn't just a save, it's free information about exactly where your process needs a permanent check.

Treating an embarrassing internal bug as the founding case for a permanent safeguard, instead of something to quietly forget, that's a decision to be honest with the team about what almost went wrong.
