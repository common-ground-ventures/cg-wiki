Wiki/Stories/Vertically integrating so nobody could point fingers but him

I started Tellus in 2004 as a green construction company. As it grew I had to decide whether to stay a pure builder who subcontracted design and labor out, or bring the whole vertical in house. I vertically integrated, architecture, design, and labor all in house, instead of staying a general contractor coordinating outside designers and subs. Owning the whole vertical removes the finger pointing that happens between a GC, a designer, and a manufacturer when something goes wrong. If you control every seam, there's nowhere for someone else's incentives to hurt your client, and there's nowhere for you to hide either. The firm scaled to roughly forty-five concurrent projects and held top Google search rankings for about a decade. The same model, own the whole vertical so incentives stay aligned, is the template I reused later structuring modular construction work and acquisition deals. Vertical integration isn't empire building. It's removing the seams where someone else's incentives can hurt your client. Every seam I removed was a place a client would otherwise have had to trust three separate parties instead of one person who was actually accountable.
