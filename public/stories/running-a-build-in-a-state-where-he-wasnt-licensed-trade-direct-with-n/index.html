Wiki/Stories/Running a build in a state where he wasn't licensed, trade-direct, with no GC

A performance car manufacturer needed its Las Vegas facility relocation delivered, but Tellus wasn't licensed as a contractor in Nevada. I structured the delivery as owner's representative and construction manager instead, coordinating every sub directly and carrying cost control, scheduling, and city interface myself, instead of turning the job away or bringing in an unrelated GC. The missing Nevada license was a licensing fact, not a capability fact. I already knew how to run subs, control cost, and manage a schedule. The missing piece was a title, and the owner's rep structure let me deliver the same outcome without needing it. The build opened on time, trade direct, saving upwards of three hundred thousand dollars versus a traditional GC structure. The same kind of long relationship with a city is what separately won us one of the only construction exemptions granted when Carson froze all new construction. A missing license is a structural problem, and structural problems have structural solutions. You don't have to be the GC of record to run the job like one. I built enough trust with the city and the client over years that when a hard rule, the freeze, the licensing gap, got in the way, people who knew me made room for a solution.
