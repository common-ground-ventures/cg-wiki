Building the bench instead of doing it alone

Wiki/Stories/Building the bench instead of doing it alone

After nineteen years running Tellus mostly by myself across every function, I started what became Common Ground Ventures. I could've kept operating as a solo principal, advising on deals the way I always had.

Instead I assembled a five-principal operating group, instead of continuing to sell my own single skill set, and I deliberately recruited partners whose careers had been built in institutions and industries far from construction.

A single operator can only credibly cover the ground his own career's actually touched. If the group's going to solve real capital, brand, and technology problems for clients, it needs principals who've actually run those functions at real size, not a generalist claiming expertise he hasn't earned.

Common Ground now runs as a principal-led operating group with real institutional, consumer-brand, and technology track records under one bench, and pitches itself as a group of operators who work embedded inside a client's business rather than advising from a deck.

The fastest way to widen what you can credibly offer a client isn't to learn five new disciplines yourself. It's to find partners who already have real records in the disciplines you don't.

This is a decision about trust. Choosing to share credit and equity with people who could operate independently, because the combined group could do more for a client than any one of us alone.
