The nine-month method

Common Ground · Stories

I left college on purpose, no plan anyone around me would've called sensible.

Wiki/Stories/The nine-month method

I left college on purpose, no plan anyone around me would've called sensible. I believed I'd learn faster by working than by sitting in a classroom, and I had a specific list of things I was bad at. Selling face to face, closing, managing money, managing people.

I picked a sequence of jobs on purpose, nine months each, and chose each one for the exact skill I thought I was worst at. Two stretches selling cars, then selling mortgages, then AAA insurance. I treated a resume gap as a curriculum instead of a failure.

The logic was simple: if a skill is weak, the fastest way to fix it is to put yourself in a room where that skill is the only thing that gets you paid. Not read about it, not wait for a job to force it on you. Nine months was long enough to see whether I was actually improving, and short enough that a bad fit didn't become a career.

Each stretch built on the last. By the time I got to AAA I set a California record for auto-policy sales inside my first three months.

A gap on paper can be the most deliberate part of a career, if you're the one who designed it.

Nobody sent me this plan. It came from a young man's own read of his own weaknesses, at an age when most people are following whatever path's put in front of them. I decided what I needed to learn and went and got it, on my own clock.
