Nine months of cold calls for a town with no budget

An EF5 tornado had flattened Greensburg, Kansas, and the rebuild effort had no money for the kind of high-performance materials the thirteen planned homes would need.

Instead of scaling back the material spec to fit no budget, I spent about nine months cold-calling national manufacturers directly, asking them to donate materials rather than sell them.

A manufacturer won't fund a town's rebuild out of charity alone. But a highly visible, nationally covered rebuild of a destroyed American town is a brand association most consumer manufacturers can't buy at any price. The pitch was never a handout, it was a trade. Material for exposure and mission alignment, made directly to the people who could say yes.

I negotiated the supplier partnerships behind more than $30M, at wholesale value, of donated sustainable materials for thirteen high-performance homes and a set of commercial buildings, with DuPont the largest of the donors. Discovery Channel, Planet Green, and Build It Bigger all covered it on camera, and honestly, I'd call it the best business development of my career.

When there's no budget, the deal you're actually offering a partner is rarely money. It's a story they want their name attached to. Find that story and make the ask directly.

This took nine months of being told no by people who owed me nothing, on behalf of a town I had no prior connection to, because I believed the work mattered. That kind of sustained, unpaid effort on someone else's behalf, that's a human choice, not a contractual obligation.
