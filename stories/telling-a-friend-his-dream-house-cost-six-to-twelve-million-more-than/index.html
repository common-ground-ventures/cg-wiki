Wiki/Stories/Telling a friend his dream house cost six to twelve million more than the market would pay back

A personal friend asked me to help a couple navigate their general contractor's budget on a custom mountain home, at a schematic design stage where the vertical construction alone was pricing well above comparable homes in the same club. I built a full independent estimate honestly, at the real number the plans implied, and delivered the build to value gap directly instead of softening it to protect the friendship or the relationship with the GC. A friend asking for help with a decision like that deserves the real number, not a comfortable one. Telling someone their home will cost roughly six to twelve million dollars more than the market comps support is a hard thing to say, but it's the only version of help that actually protects them. I delivered the full estimate package, including the build to value gap, as a friend to friend working document rather than a formal proposal, so the owners had a real basis for their own decision before construction locked in. The kindest thing you can do for a friend making a huge financial decision is tell them the number that's hard to hear, before it's too late to change course. I chose honesty over comfort with someone I actually cared about, trusting the friendship could hold the hard number better than it could hold a bad surprise later.
