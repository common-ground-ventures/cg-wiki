Wiki/Stories/The keyhole

Asked how much of his history was ready to ship, the system reported roughly twenty engagements with usable backup. He pushed back: 'I've touched way more than what you're showing here. I think you're looking at it the wrong way.'

Stop trusting the folder taxonomy and sweep three independent sources instead: the resume and public profiles, the ledger's unexplored eras, and the disk itself including the mail archive.

The search had been scoped to where the filing system said client work belonged. But the filing system was built in 2026 and the work spans 2001 to now. Anything predating the current taxonomy was invisible by construction, not by absence. A count produced by searching only the places a category expects is a measure of the category, not of the record.

One hundred twenty-nine distinct engagements surfaced against the twenty reported. The real archive was in two folders nobody had queried: old business records and corporate records. Two shuttered ventures turned out to have monthly financial statements and tax returns already on disk. An NBA practice facility build was sitting in the ledger as a single unread line.

When a search returns less than the person expects, suspect the scope before the substance. An inventory is only as wide as the assumptions used to build it.

He knew his own history and said so plainly rather than accepting a flattering-sounding number. The correction cost one sentence and recovered a hundred engagements.
