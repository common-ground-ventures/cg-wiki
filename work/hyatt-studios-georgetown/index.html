An extended-stay hotel in central Texas: a feasibility study that split the question in two

Work · Development

Feasibility study for a branded extended-stay hotel in central Texas: an honest no in February, then a trade-by-trade estimate the developer is raising against.

Wiki/Work/Extended-Stay Hotel, Central Texas

What it is

A hotel developer planned a four-story, branded extended-stay hotel in central Texas. It had been in due diligence for well over a year, its market report had gotten worse, and it had already cut its own fees close to nothing. Its capital model needed hard cost well under the schematic budget, a gap no value-engineering pass could close, and it wanted to know whether Xtrata's SABS panel wall system could close it. The question reached Fowler in February 2026 through LÏEF's standing seat with Xtrata.

LÏEF Development ran the construction review and the coordination with a Texas general contractor that joined in April. Common Ground ran the feasibility study with Xtrata. The work was advisory and non-exclusive: estimates, cost analysis, construction review and coordination.

What Common Ground did

  • Took the original budget apart line by line and asked what share of the building the panels could physically touch. The lines the system replaces outright, and the lines it partly replaces, came to a small share of the schematic budget. A hotel is mostly everywhere else: plumbing, HVAC, electrical, finishes, site work and elevators.
  • Put it in writing in February that the panel swap alone could not close the gap. Even counting labor savings on other trades, the realistic system impact still left the budget well over target. Texas also had no trained SABS crews, no distributor and no inspectors who had seen the system, so Common Ground recommended against stretching the system to hit the number.
  • Re-opened the study in April, when a 65 percent design development set and a Texas general contractor with a Texas trade network came to the table. Every trade was repriced to the central Texas market, and an April 14 figure, plus or minus 12 percent, went out with its one load-bearing assumption named in writing.
  • Reviewed the Texas contractor's budget before trusting it. It appeared to hit the target, but the panel material line was blank and the contractor's profit had been removed. With the material added back on a like-for-like basis it came in well over, and the easy yes was gone.
  • Built a three-way, line-by-line comparison of all three budgets through Kanopi, LÏEF's estimating engine, with a quantity and a rate on every line, and priced the final estimate trade by trade. The panel material sat on its own line as a pass-through, not marked up, so a lender could check it against the manufacturer's price. The band on the estimate ran minus 3 percent to plus 6 percent, weighted to the upside because Texas subcontractor pricing was soft.
  • Named the gates the estimate could not close: capital, the hotel brand's review of the panel system, a first SABS permit submittal in the jurisdiction with a four to six month timeline, a design development set rather than construction documents, a soft subcontractor market, and HVAC sized down for the panel envelope but not yet signed by a mechanical engineer. The construction answer was kept separate from the capital answer.

Results

  • A final trade-by-trade estimate went to the developer on May 27, 2026, close to its target.
  • The final landed under both the original budget and the Texas contractor's normalized budget; most of the gap to the Texas budget sat in mechanical and electrical. The estimate figures stay with the developer and behind the data room's gate.
  • The construction question was answered to the dollar. The capital question stayed with the developer, which is raising the money against the estimate.
  • A standing review step came out of the study: normalize a partner's budget before believing it, then set it beside the original and beside LÏEF's own, division by division. The central Texas trade pricing became the reference set for the next hotel or multifamily estimate, and LÏEF estimates now carry no round line items.
